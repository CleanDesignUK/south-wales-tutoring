WJEC GCSE Maths & Numeracy Double Award: A Parent Guide

Understand the new WJEC GCSE Mathematics and Numeracy Double Award, how to check your child’s qualification and how to plan useful revision.

South Wales Tutoring  ·  11 November 2026  ·  5 min read

In this guide

  2. Find the earliest gap
  3. Build a revision routine
  4. When tutoring can help

Families looking for GCSE Maths tutoring in South Wales may have noticed a change in the qualifications their children are studying. The new WJEC GCSE Mathematics and Numeracy (Double Award) is one of the Made-for-Wales qualifications introduced for teaching from September 2025. It is important to check the specific course, year group and tier before choosing revision materials.

1. Check the qualification before buying revision books

Do not assume that an older “GCSE Mathematics” past paper matches the current assessment. Ask the school for the qualification name or check the student’s course information. WJEC publishes the official Mathematics and Numeracy Double Award page, including the specification and sample assessment materials.

The new course connects numerical fluency, mathematical language, reasoning and practical application. WJEC identifies number, algebra, geometry and measures, and statistics and probability as core mathematical concepts. For a learner, this means practice should include explanation and interpretation, not only repetitive calculations.

2. Find the earliest gap, not just the hardest question

Suppose a student struggles to solve an algebra problem. It might be because of negative numbers, multiplying fractions, rearranging equations or understanding what a letter represents. A good tutoring session works back to the missing skill and then returns to the original problem.

One useful approach is to choose eight to ten questions from mixed topics and ask the student to explain how they worked. An incorrect answer is useful evidence. So is an answer they get right by copying a memorised pattern they cannot explain.

3. Combine short revision sessions with questions

A practical weekly routine can include one short review of an older topic, a focused explanation of a difficult idea, several independent questions and a quick reflection. Students benefit from spaced practice more than a last-minute rush through entire chapters.

  • Number: fractions, percentages and unit conversions.
  • Algebra: expressions, equations and interpreting graphs.
  • Geometry: measures, angles, area and reasoning.
  • Statistics: representing and interpreting data.

Use the student’s actual specification to decide which examples and styles of assessment are most relevant.

4. When a tutor could help

A Maths tutor can be helpful when the learner repeatedly makes the same error, avoids questions or cannot explain why an answer is correct. The goal should be to build independent understanding, not simply finish a worksheet during the session.
